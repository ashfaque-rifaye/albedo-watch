# Albedo-Watch: demo script for recording yourself

Target length 4:30 to 4:55 (the limit is 5:00). About 680 spoken words. Numbers on screen change with live data, so where a line has [brackets], say what your screen shows.

## Before you hit record

1. Open https://albedo-watch-621000818329.asia-south1.run.app/app five minutes early so the server is awake. Click through Detect (India), Trace (Panipat), Protect (Delhi) and Accuracy once, so they load fast later.
2. Allow location when Chrome asks. Then click **change** on your hub card so it goes back to **⌖ Use my location**. Chrome remembers the permission, so there is no pop-up on camera.
3. Skip the first-run tour (**Skip** or Esc). Leave live reports on (the **◉** button on the right).
4. **Switch the map to *Open data*** (bottom-left). Google's Map Tiles policy allows promotional videos that show Google map tiles only if they are 30 seconds or shorter and show no Street View. In *Open data* mode every Google map feature is off, so the full demo can run for 5 minutes. If you want to show Google's 3D or satellite tiles, make a separate clip of 30 seconds or less, with no Street View, marked "For promotional purposes only".
5. Have a real photo of smoke or burning that you took yourself ready on the computer. Don't use AI or internet images: the app checks authenticity, and it wouldn't be honest.
6. Chrome full screen (F11), zoom 100%, 1920×1080, other tabs closed, Windows notifications off (Focus assist).
7. Recorder: OBS, Xbox Game Bar (Win+Alt+R) or Loom, 1080p. Record scene by scene. Gemini takes 10 to 90 seconds in Trace, Citizen, Command and Copilot: keep recording, then cut the wait, leaving a second of the step list so people see it working.
8. Read the lines aloud once and change any wording that doesn't sound like you. They are a guide, not a teleprompter.

## 0. Opening (0:00–0:20)

**Show:** the globe, zoomed out, Pulse panel open. Don't touch anything for two seconds.

> Hi, I'm [your name], and this is Albedo-Watch. India has roughly 550 continuous air monitors for 1.4 billion people. So most of the air we breathe is never measured. And when it does get bad, the warning comes late, and it doesn't reach the person who can do something about it. That's the gap I built this for.

## 1. Pulse: the air near you (0:20–0:55)

**Show:**
1. Drag the globe so India is in the middle.
2. Top-left lens buttons: click **HAZE**, wait three seconds, click **TRUE**.
3. In the Pulse panel, click **⌖ Use my location**.
4. When your hub card loads, under *My air plan* click **Asthma / COPD**.
5. Point at *Best time outside* and at **🔔 Alert me before bad air**. Only click it if you want the browser prompt on camera.

> Everything on this globe is live. 233 cities, each on its own country's index, the fires NASA's satellites picked up in the last day, and today's winds. This HAZE button swaps in NASA's picture of smoke and dust from space.
>
> If I tap "Use my location", I get the air forecast for my street for the next 24 hours. Say I have asthma: it tells me the best time to be outside, and it'll send me an alert before the air here turns bad.

## 2. Any place, in 3D (0:55–1:30)

**Show:**
1. Press **◍** (Whole Earth) on the right if you need to, then scroll-zoom into Delhi and click the centre of Connaught Place. Backup: open `/app?mode=place&lat=28.6315&lon=77.2167`.
2. Wait for the place panel to fill: forecast, nearby fires, sensors and monitors, satellite image.
3. Click **◳ 3D city**. When the buildings are in, drag slowly to orbit.
4. Don't open Street View in this recording (see step 4 of the preparation).

> I can click anywhere on Earth. This is Connaught Place in Delhi. I get the forecast for this exact spot, the fires nearby, the closest sensors and official monitors, and today's satellite image. On the normal Google map it also shows Google's live air quality and Street View.
>
> Hit "3D city" and the buildings come up from OpenStreetMap. The haze isn't decoration. It's calculated from the PM2.5 forecast at this spot right now, so this is roughly how far you'd actually see today.

## 3. Detect (1:30–1:55)

**Show:**
1. Top bar: **Detect**, then **India**.
2. Click **#1** under *Ranked hidden hotspots*. The globe flies there.
3. In the card, point at the detection count, *to nearest official monitor* and the confidence badge, then scroll to the satellite image.

> Detect finds pollution nobody's measuring. It takes NASA's fire detections, checks them against every official monitor on OpenAQ, and ignores fires where the air is clean anyway. This one's in [district, state]. The satellites keep seeing it, the nearest official monitor is [over 100 km] away, and here's the satellite image.

## 4. Forecast (1:55–2:07)

**Show:**
1. Top bar: **Forecast**. Let the corridor cards show.
2. Right-hand buttons: click **◷** (Forecast replay). Let it play about five seconds, then **Close**.

> Forecast looks 72 hours ahead along India's main corridors, so a spike in Punjab warns Delhi before it gets there. This button plays the whole forecast out on the globe.

## 5. Trace (2:07–2:42)

**Show:**
1. Top bar: **Trace**. In the city dropdown pick **Panipat** (or Delhi).
2. The steps run while Gemini writes the explanation. Cut the wait.
3. Show the headline, the source bar and the upwind fire list.
4. Scroll to **Act: response simulator**. Tick one more measure and drag **Enforcement compliance**. Stop on the *Worth, over a 3-day episode* box. If that box doesn't appear, tick more measures.

> Trace answers the question everyone argues about every winter: where's this coming from? It runs the air backwards 48 hours through the wind, sees which fires it passed over, and splits the PM2.5 by source. Gemini explains it in plain English.
>
> Then there's the part an official actually needs. Pick the measures, set how well they'll be enforced, and it shows how much cleaner the air gets, the deaths and hospital admissions that avoids, and what that's worth in rupees. They're estimates, and it says so.

## 6. Citizen (2:42–3:07)

**Show:**
1. Top bar: **Citizen**.
2. Click **Photo** and pick your real photo. Or click **Voice** and say a sentence in Hindi.
3. Language dropdown: **हिन्दी**. Click **⌖ My location**, or **Tap globe** and click where the photo was taken.
4. Click **Send report**. Cut the wait.
5. On the result, show the status and source, the *Independent verification* meters, *Routed to*, the reply in Hindi, then **▶ Hear it (Gemini voice)**.

> Anyone can report. I'll send a photo of burning I saw near [place]. Gemini works out what's burning and checks it's a real photo, and separately we check it against the satellites. It goes to the office responsible for that district, and I get a reply in Hindi that I can listen to. It also pops up as a live report for everyone else on the map.

## 7. Protect (3:07–3:27)

**Show:**
1. Top bar: **Protect**. Pick **Delhi**.
2. Yellow squares (schools) and pink crosses (hospitals) appear on the globe. Point at the two counts and *Today, hour by hour*.
3. Click **▲ Advisory to school principals**.

> Protect is for the people who feel it first. In Delhi it finds about 750 schools and 740 hospitals and clinics, and turns the forecast into hours when children can be outside and hours to keep them in. One click drafts an advisory for principals.

## 8. Command (3:27–3:57)

**Show:**
1. You land in Command on *New order · Delhi · school principals*. Languages: English and Hindi. Add another with **+ add** if you like. Leave the satellite-image box ticked.
2. Click **✦ Draft with Gemini**. Cut the wait.
3. Show the title, the *To:* line and the advisory, then switch the advisory to हिन्दी.
4. Scroll down, click **Approve →**, then **Dispatch →**. Show the ledger.

> This is Command. Gemini drafts it for the right authority, in English and Hindi, and it reads today's satellite image as evidence. Nothing goes out on its own. An officer reads it and approves it, and every step is logged. In this prototype the sending itself is simulated, but the approval trail is real.

## 9. Albedo Copilot (3:57–4:27)

**Show:**
1. Click **✦ Copilot** (top right).
2. Click the first suggestion, *"Which schools in Delhi should keep children indoors tomorrow? Draft a notice to principals in Hindi."* Or press **●** and ask it out loud.
3. Cut the wait. Show the tool steps and the answer while the map flies to Delhi, opens Protect, then opens the draft in Command. Close with ×.

> Everything I just clicked through, you can also just ask for. This is Albedo Copilot, a Gemini agent that uses the same tools. You can see each step: it finds Delhi, checks the forecast, counts the schools and writes the notice, and the map follows along. It still stops at a draft that a person has to sign.

If the steps it shows are different, describe the ones on screen.

## 10. Accuracy (4:27–4:45)

**Show:**
1. Top bar: **Accuracy**. India loads by default, each state marked ▼ or ▲.
2. Point at a green state, then an amber one, then the big percentage.

> Last question: can you trust the forecast? Each state corrects the global model with its own monitors and only shares what it learned, never the data. Across India that cuts the error by about [25] percent today. And where it doesn't help yet, like here, it tells you.

## 11. Close (4:45–4:57)

**Show:** click **◍** (Whole Earth) and let the globe turn.

> It's live on Google Cloud, built on Gemini and Google Maps, and a new state or country joins by adding data, not code. That's Albedo-Watch. Thanks for watching.
